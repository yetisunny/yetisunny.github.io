# Personal Development 
So I started studying in 2018, a wide eyed freshman, fully intending to complete my bachelors in 3 years time, and the masters in 2. Obviously though,  I am writing these words in 2025, which is quite a ways off from that original estimate.  

When I started studying I had expected to learn so many interesting things, and my hopes for university were high, and they were met in large part at first. Sure there were times when I felt like the things I was studying weren't particularly relevant to "real life", but the material was engaging nonetheless. However,  obviously not everything was equally engrossing. My interests don't lie in formal logic for instance, but having learned it has without question been beneficial to me in some immeasurable way. 
## The elephant in the room
Now, my first two years of study went very well, I think the only course I did
not pass might have been functional programming, you might wonder then how I was led so astray. Mind you, what I am about to put to paper is likely not
the whole truth or reason of it, because I still don't understand it, that likely would require some form of therapy, alas.

It all started going wrong during the covid-lockdowns. The entire online-learning spiel did not do me much good. Furthermore I had just built a whole routine around working out, and that fell away as well which hurt my mental well-being immensely.

I easily fell into the trap of skipping online lectures ( and later physical lectures as well ) under the guise of "watching them back later", which, you obviously don't do, because who are you kidding? Then those build up and up,and the metaphorical obstacle has now grown to such a degree where it is far easier to just throw your hands up and say "Nevermind, I will just do it next year". This went on for some years, where I for whatever reason couldn't get myself to work on university. Maybe it is partially a fear of failure? For if you don't try you can't fail either. 

A reason that also speaks to me is the fact that I did not really learn much interesting stuff towards the end of my study, I felt as though I was just doing it to get the piece of paper, but my engagement was not really there for much of it. I was doing courses with first years students who could not relate with me very well. 

Another big factor in my taking so long was the fact that I got a side-job as a software developer/It consultant at a small company nearby, this led to my not having to borrow any additional money to continue studying, and led to lower pressure on myself to finish. I worked there for almost 2 and a half years I believe, and then left to work elsewhere also a .software developer, with Martijn Vastenburg, whom is also a lecturer at Radoud.

# Courses
So when I first started studying my interest were always towards artificial intelligence, which is still true in large part today. I thought the courses related to this such as big data, data mining, intro to AI, etc I found the most interesting. I'd say that my interests still lie there, but in a subtly  different way. I would like to focus my further studies on methods that can be used for more good than just generating AI slop. 

I chose the Data Science track and I am pretty satisfied with the choices for the most part, I started of with the Artificial intelligence minor. I did not finish a certain course, which I had to redo completly if I wanted the credits, I did not feel like doing that so I ended up creating a custom program.  The program included a course called "Terrorisme Beeld en werkelijkheid", which I heard was easy credits, so I took that. By this point in my study I just wanted to get any credits so I could get a degree.

# Educational Organisation
As far as the third year is concerned, I don't fully recall a lot of details or things that stand out to me. I think the whole setup of leaving a lot of choice is good, but can also in some circumstances lead to worse outcomes. It's weirdly nice sometimes when the path is just laid out for you, instead of having to choose. However, I will say that if you pick your courses right then it should be perfectly feasible to do in 1 year, but I am defintely the wrong person to ask about this.

About the bachelor as a whole, I think the program is mostly fine. It has been so long since I took many of these courses I am finding it a little difficult to bring to mind very concrete examples of courses I thought were exceptionally bad, or good. I might say that some courses like "Requirements engineering" are a little much, as in real life I haven't found this to be super applicable beyond the obvious "Make sure you know exactly what people want".

The program is alright, I won't pretend to know how to make it very much better as  I don't particularly see any insane flaws that aren't shared by any other university degree. What I mean by that is that everything you learn in university is going to be applied differently in the real world, the nice standards or ways of doing things tend to go out of the window. The most important part of university is that you learn to think better, and learn to learn, which I think the program does pretty well, provided you don't use chatGPT for everything (which at the time when I did it wasn't possible, which is a good thing I'd say. )

## course rankings:
I shall provide a course ranking of three "third year" courses that I found most interesting, to the best of my ability:

"Big Data", because it showed a practical way of actually working with big data, and Arjen is a great lecturer.

"Introduction to robotics", because messing around with lego robots was a lot of fun.

"Deep learning", which I took as an elective I think (It may have been part of the "minor" ), was very interesting because I just find the material engaging.

I think the course I found most interesting in the entire bachelors my favourite might be introduction to robotics, or new devices lab, just because they are practically fun.

The least interesting ones:

"Semantics and correctness", I found it hard, it wasn't bad, but just that and it's not my interest.
"Terrorisme beeld en Werkelijk", the lecturer wanted you to buy and read her book, but most of the topics were fairly common sense anyway, so it wasn't the best.

"Theoretical Modeling for Cogn. Science", the material was okay but I just found the whole thing to be less engaging that I had thought at first. 

Frankly the "least" ranked coures weren't bad , but if I am forced to think of 3 courses and rank them, then that's probably the ones.

# Study progress
As of writing this, the only remaining credits I need are from the course for which I am writing this very reflection, so 117.  There  is not much more to it than that :)

My plans for after this are to finally start with a clean slate on the data science master. I am looking forward to doing courses, going to lectures about stuff that interests me. I do intend to keep working alongside it though, part time.  

You might find the choice to continue studying strange, given the fact the bachelor took me so long, and my general opinion that degrees aren't all that they are made out to be. However, I still enjoy learning, and if I do get a masters degree, a lot of people will think me more competent, which is always welcome.

In short, I think starting the study with the intention of doing it in 3 years, but then taking far far longer than that has made me into a better person in the end. If I could go back in time, would I make certain other decisions? Of course, but none of us have that luxury, so I will just try to learn from it. Life always tends to take a different path than you might have initially expected,but you can always try to start over, which I intend to try for the masters.
